Research Agenda

My research studies how financial institutions, regulatory frameworks, and political coalitions shape the transition toward sustainable and climate-aligned economies. Environmental politics is fundamentally about competing material interests and visions of the future asking who wins and who loses in the shift away from carbon-intensive growth, and what institutional arrangements might better align financial incentives with ecological limits.

My empirical research focuses on three interconnected domains. First, how do central bank policies shape climate risk pricing and investor incentives? Second, how do supply-side regulations like the EU Deforestation Regulation or green bond standards alter investor behavior and reduce greenwashing? Third, what role do food systems and agricultural finance play in environmental transitions? Capital markets have remained largely indifferent to agricultural sustainability despite its critical importance for climate mitigation and adaptation.

I specialize in computational and quasi-experimental methods to answer these questions. Underlying my work is a theoretical interest in how institutions mediate the relationship between markets, states, and environmental outcomes. Environmental sustainability is not a technical problem solved just by having better metrics, but a fundamentally political question requiring transformation of politico-economic institutions.
